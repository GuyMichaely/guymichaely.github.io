Guy Michaely's Opinions

Why don't you experience a sense of touch when I touch my own arm?

When I touch my arm, I feel my arm being touched. Why don't you feel that sensation? And why don't I feel it when your arm is touched?

When you touch your arm, your body sends nerve signals to your brain and not to mine. Naturally, then, I don't feel your touch. But where's the connection between brain activity and a particular conscious experience? I'll grant that your brain only produces experience for your body, but why is your experience "yours"? Why don't I experience your sensations, embodied in your body?

My response

Assuming physicalism1 and taking it as a given that Guy exists and that I am him:

  • Guy's body produces a conscious experience; the two are a closed system
  • Part of the experience that Guy's body produces is a sense of "I". That sense is merely a piece of content within that experience. Most of the time, Guy's sense of "I" comes with a feeling of identity with Guy's body and with Guy more generally. Incidentally, it's possible for Guy's sense of "I" not to have a feeling of identity with Guy; depersonalization and psychosis are both plausible examples
  • Any given subject using the word "I" uses it relative to their sense of "I", which is populated by the body into the subject's experience

Why we don't feel each other's arms

When I touch my arm:

  • Nerves send signals to my brain
  • My brain produces two qualia:
    • The sensation of arm touching
    • A sense of "I": A feeling of identity, with ideas that this "I" is tied to Guy

Since your body isn't my body, your body doesn't produce the arm sensation that mine does. I identify with the sensation because of my sense of "I". Even if I didn't identify with the sensation, it would nonetheless be present in my subjective experience and not present in yours. Thus, I feel my body, you feel yours, and neither of us feels each other's.
